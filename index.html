About

My goal is to build safe AI systems for diverse biomedical applications. I combine safety-critical systems engineering, biology and computational biology to develop end-to-end discovery workflows.

Most people work as either a biologist or an engineer. For over a decade I have worked between the two: I build AI and computational pipelines, then go into the lab to test their predictions. My background spans immunology, haematology and oncology, and I have worked in academia, pharma R&D and start-ups.

What I do

Four threads run through my work.

Agentic discovery, validated at the bench

In pharma R&D I build proof-of-concept agentic workflows for early discovery, in which AI agents assemble the analysis pipelines and prioritised targets go on to lab validation. I then work with AI and engineering teams to take them to production scale.

Agentic AITarget discoveryLab validation

Evidence for decisions

With business development teams, I evaluate external assets and opportunities. I build target opportunity packages that pull together omics and other data to inform in-licensing decisions.

Due diligenceMulti-omicsBiomarkers

Test-first, safety-minded engineering

My PhD, run between immunology and a safety-critical systems engineering group, developed a test-driven approach to building multi-agent systems. Acceptance tests were derived from requirements, and arguments showed each model was fit for purpose. Predictions were then validated in the lab.

Teaching scientists to use AI

I lead AI training for the British Society for Immunology, a nationwide programme teaching biologists to build their own AI workflows. I have also taught at Institut Pasteur, ENS, Sorbonne, Oxford and Imperial.

Selected publications

15 peer-reviewed publications. The full list is on Google Scholar.

  1. The pentose phosphate pathway regulates myelo-lymphoid lineage specification
    Cosgrove J, Donada A, Lyne A, et al. Nature Communications (accepted, 2026)
    Metabolic state-fate mapping shows that stem cell metabolism can be targeted to steer immune cell production, validated in vivo.
  2. A call for accessible tools to unlock single-cell immunometabolism research
    Cosgrove J, et al. Nature Metabolism (2024)
    A first-author perspective, written with leading international immunometabolism labs, on the tools the field needs.
  3. Breast cancer remotely imposes a myeloid bias on haematopoietic stem cells by reprogramming the bone marrow niche
    Gerber-Ferder Y, Cosgrove J, et al. Nature Cell Biology (2023)
    Tumours reprogramme the bone marrow niche from a distance, pushing stem cells towards pro-tumour myeloid output.
  4. B cell zone reticular cell microenvironments shape CXCL13 gradient formation
    Cosgrove J, Novkovic M, et al. Nature Communications (2020)
    PhD paper: agent-based simulation and optimisation of immune cell dynamics, with predictions validated by imaging and knockout mice.
  5. Using emulation to engineer and understand simulations of biological systems
    Alden K, Cosgrove J, Coles M, Timmis J. IEEE/ACM Trans. Comput. Biol. Bioinform. (2020)
    A machine-learning emulator workflow that makes evaluating multi-agent simulations far faster, enabling multi-objective optimisation and Bayesian uncertainty analysis.
  6. Agent-based modeling in systems pharmacology
    Cosgrove J, Butler J, Alden K, et al. CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology (2015)
    A tutorial introducing agent-based modelling to pharmacologists, with preclinical drug-discovery case studies.
